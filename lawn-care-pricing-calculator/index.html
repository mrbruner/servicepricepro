Pricing guide

How to Price Lawn Care Jobs as a Beginner

Pricing lawn care jobs is not just about the size of the yard. Beginners should also think about grass height, trimming, edging, obstacles, travel time, fuel, equipment wear, and how much profit they need to keep after expenses.

Start with the job size

A small easy yard may be simple, but a large yard with obstacles can take much longer.

Add difficulty and extras

Overgrown grass, trimming, edging, bagging, and cleanup should raise the price because they add labor.

Protect your profit

The goal is not to be the cheapest. The goal is to charge a fair price that covers your time, costs, and profit.

Beginner mistakes

Common Lawn Care Pricing Mistakes

Avoid these mistakes before you quote the next job.

1. Charging too low just to win the jobLow prices can hurt if they do not cover fuel, time, equipment wear, and profit.
2. Forgetting travel timeDriving still costs money and time, even if you are not mowing yet.
3. Not charging extra for overgrown grassTall or thick grass usually takes longer and can be harder on equipment.
4. Including trimming and edging for freeExtra services should increase the price because they add time and labor.
5. Ignoring equipment wearMowers, trimmers, blades, oil, and maintenance all cost money over time.
6. Only looking at revenue, not profitA $60 job is not really $60 profit if you spent money to complete it.
FAQ

Lawn Care Pricing Questions

Simple answers before you quote.

How much should a beginner charge to mow a lawn?

A beginner should charge enough to cover labor time, travel, fuel, equipment wear, job difficulty, and profit. Small simple jobs may cost less, while large or overgrown yards should cost more.

What affects lawn care pricing the most?

The biggest factors are yard size, grass height, job difficulty, trimming, edging, bagging, travel time, and expenses.

Should I charge by the hour or by the yard?

Beginners can use both. Yard size gives a starting point, but estimated labor time helps protect profit when a job takes longer than expected.

Should I charge extra for overgrown grass?

Yes. Overgrown grass usually takes more time, fuel, and effort, so it should cost more than a normal maintenance mow.

Should travel time be included in lawn care pricing?

Yes. Travel time should be included because driving uses time, fuel, vehicle wear, and unpaid work time.

Is this calculator a guaranteed local market price?

No. This calculator gives a beginner-friendly estimate, not a guaranteed local market price. Always adjust for your city, job difficulty, expenses, and customer expectations.
